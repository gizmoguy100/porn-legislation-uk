# Dossier 05 — Other loopholes and reform proposals in the UK Online Safety Act age-verification regime

**Prepared:** 3 November 2026  
**Scope:** Part 5 (ss 79–82) “regulated provider pornographic content”; Part 3 (ss 12, 26–29) children’s safety duties; Ofcom enforcement; the Independent Review of Pornography; parliamentary and NGO scrutiny; reform vehicles; litigation; international comparison.  
**Excluded:** the US 4chan case (covered by another subagent).  
**Method:** targeted web searches, parliamentary and regulator publications, PDF extraction of the Bertin review, and cross-checking of secondary reporting against primary sources. Where a claim rests only on secondary reporting or an industry source, that is flagged.  
**Cut-off:** 3 November 2026.

> **Bottom line:** the most important 2025–26 “loopholes” are not primarily about weak face-estimation technology. They are structural: search services are expressly outside the HEAA duty; Part 5 only reaches “provider” porn and excludes text-only, artistic and educational material; the long tail and offshore operators remain largely untouched; app stores, operating systems and devices are outside the Act; and payment/affiliate infrastructure is unregulated. The regime has changed traffic patterns and produced geo-blocking, but it has also displaced demand to unchecked sites and created new compliance theatre.

---

## Headline findings

1. **Search is the biggest single statutory gap.** Ofcom states plainly that search services are **not** required to use HEAA to prevent children accessing pornography, while 33% of first-page Google results and 54% of first-page Bing results in its test were to porn sites without age checks.
2. **Part 5 is narrow.** It covers porn “published or displayed” by the provider, not user-generated content; text-only content is excluded; artistic and educational content can fall outside the definition; education providers are exempt.
3. **The long tail is the market.** Ofcom has identified at least **20,000** adult sites; enforcement is concentrated on the top 100; 47% of the porn services visited by children in its passive measurement study had **no age checks**.
4. **Offshore enforcement is real but shallow.** Ofcom had issued **32 formal notices** and fined **7 providers/31 services** by July 2026, but had made **zero ISP blocking orders**; it acknowledges limits where overseas companies have few UK assets.
5. **Device-level assurance is the new battleground.** Aylo partially reopened Pornhub to UK users in May 2026 using Apple’s device-level age signal; Ofcom opened an investigation in September 2026 because responsibility remains with the service, not Apple.
6. **App stores and operating systems are outside the Act.** Ofcom’s 29 October 2026 statutory report says app stores “could do more” but are not regulated services; the Government has not yet decided whether to bring them into scope.
7. **AI companions and nudify tools are the fastest-moving gaps.** Ofcom is investigating Novi; the Government plans under-18 restrictions on sexualised chatbots by 2027; ISD found 163 of 181 nudify sites accessible from the UK, only 2 with age verification.
8. **Reform is fragmented.** The Bertin review’s 32 recommendations remain largely unimplemented; the under-16 social media ban is the Government’s flagship; the OSA’s own statutory review under s 178 may not begin until 2029 and could be as late as 2032.

## 1. Structural gaps in the statutory architecture

### 1.1 Search services are expressly outside the HEAA duty

**Description:** Search engines are regulated services with illegal-content and children’s safety duties, but the Act does not require them to use HEAA to stop children reaching pornographic websites. This is the clearest “upstream” loophole in the regime.

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026** — [*Age checks helping make online experiences safer…*](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/age-checks-helping-make-online-experiences-safer-for-uk-children-but-job-not-done-and-tech-industry-must-act-to-strengthen-protections), note 9: “**Search services are not required under the Act to use highly effective age assurance to prevent access to pornographic content by children.**”
- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026** — same release: “We found that **33% of results returned on the first page of Google Search and 54% on Bing** were to pornography sites without age checks or other protections.”
- **Ofcom, 14 July 2026** — [*Children’s Passive Online Measurement — Pornography Services Report*](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/online-safety/research-statistics-and-data/protecting-children/childrens-passive-online-measurement-pornography-services-report.pdf): “Search engines accounted for around **30% of entry points** to pornography services in our study of 8–14s”; “**62%** of the children who visited pornographic services used a search engine immediately before visiting porn services at least once.”
- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026** — [*Report on the use of age assurance*](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/online-safety/information-for-industry/age-assurance-report-2026/report-on-the-use-of-age-assurance.pdf): “almost half (**47%**) of the pornography services visited by children have no age checks in place… children can find these sites via search engines.”
- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026** — Google and Bing “have agreed to work with Ofcom and other relevant stakeholders to explore practical solutions to address discoverability of porn services without protections via search engines.” This is voluntary, not a statutory duty.

**Status:** Live gap. No legislation extends HEAA to search. Voluntary industry workstream only.

**Gaps/unknowns:** What concrete changes Google/Bing have made since July 2026; whether Ofcom will use its search risk-assessment/code powers to require downranking or deindexing; whether the Government will legislate in the next online safety bill.

### 1.2 Part 5 covers only “provider” porn; text, art and education carve-outs

**Description:** Part 5 applies to “regulated provider pornographic content” — content published or displayed by the provider or someone acting for it. It does not cover user-generated content (which falls under Part 3) and contains express exclusions. The definition of “pornographic content” is purpose-based (“solely or principally for the purpose of sexual arousal”), and Ofcom’s guidance recognises that artistic or educational material may fall outside it.

**What we found**

- **Online Safety Act 2023, s 79** — [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/part/5): “**Pornographic content that is user-generated content** in relation to an internet service is **not** to be regarded as provider pornographic content.”
- **Ofcom, 13 December 2024** — [*Overview of regulated services*](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/online-safety/information-for-industry/illegal-harms/overview-of-regulated-services.pdf): provider porn “excludes content that consists **only of text**, or text accompanied by emojis or non-pornographic GIFs.”
- **Ofcom, 24 April 2025** — [*Guidance on Content Harmful to Children*](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/consultations/category-1-10-weeks/statement-protecting-children-from-harms-online/main-document/guidance-on-content-harmful-to-children.pdf): “**If the primary purpose is artistic, for example, content will not meet the definition under the Act.**” It also notes that “some artistic and educational content may meet the definitions of PPC and PC… because they are likely to be harmful to children.”
- **Ofcom, 13 December 2024** — *Overview of regulated services*: a U2U or search service is exempt if provided by a UK education or childcare provider and made available for education or childcare.
- **Substack, 29 September 2026** — [Content Guidelines](https://substack.com/content): “We don’t allow porn or sexually exploitative content… We do allow depictions of nudity for **artistic, journalistic, or related purposes**, as well as **erotic literature**.” Substack’s text/erotic-literature surface is a plausible text-only avoidance route.
- **decoded.legal, 27 November 2023** — [analysis](https://decoded.legal/blog/2023/11/online-safety-act-2023-the-uks-anti-pornographic-content-provisions): the “solely or principally” formula “potentially opens up some avenues to explore” for artistic, educational, nudist, community or review content; the blog also notes there is **no exception for individual-operated sites** such as personal sex blogs.

**Status:** Live statutory scope gap. No enforcement action found against a site claiming an artistic/educational/text-only exemption.

**Gaps/unknowns:** No public Ofcom dataset on how many sites rely on these carve-outs; no case law on “solely or principally” in the porn context; risk that the text-only exclusion becomes more important as AI-generated erotic text/audio grows (audio is expressly in scope; text is not).

### 1.3 The long tail, offshore hosting and enforcement reach

**Description:** Ofcom supervises the top of the market intensively but cannot practically police tens of thousands of adult sites. Many are offshore, have no UK corporate presence, and can ignore or evade fines. The Act’s ultimate sanction — ISP blocking — has not been used against porn sites.

**What we found**

- **Ofcom written evidence to the Lords, 7 September 2026** — [IOS0039](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/169185/html), para 4.9: “this is a dynamic market which includes a **longtail of at least 20,000 identified adult sites** and the ‘top 100’ changes often. While we have made good progress… there will inevitably remain non-compliant sites.”
- **Ofcom, 30 July 2026** — [FOI response on enforcement](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/about-ofcom/foi/2026/july/enforcement-of-age-assurance-duties-for-online-pornography-providers.pdf): **7 providers** fined, relating to **31 services**; **32 formal notices** issued; answer to Q10/Q11 on ISP-level blocking: “**the answer is zero**.”
- **The Register, 23 September 2026** — [report on Aylo probe](https://assets.theregister.com/2026/09/23/202614): Ofcom “acknowledged limits to Ofcom’s reach, particularly when companies operating from overseas have **few UK assets against which fines can be enforced**.”
- **BBC News, 9 July 2026** — [*Porn site provider fined £630,000…*](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c07ylddnvmyo): “It was revealed in December the regulator had **never heard from a firm handed a £1m fine**.”
- **Ofcom, 2025/26 Annual Report** — [PDF](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/about-ofcom/annual-reports/2025-26/ofcom-annual-report-and-accounts-2025-2026.pdf): “opening investigations into nearly 100 services, and levying **18 fines**… That included fines against **six services which failed to implement effective age checks**.”
- **Ofcom, 4 September 2026** — [Xgroovy case](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/porn-site-deploys-age-checks-as-ofcom-fines-it-730000): £700,000 for no age checks plus £30,000 for failing to respond; the provider then “**geoblocked** access from UK IP addresses.”
- **Ofcom, 9 July 2026** — [fapello case](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/ofcom-fines-porn-company-630000-for-age-check-failings): £600,000 plus £30,000; fapello “has now been ‘**geo-blocked**’ from UK IP addresses.”
- **Ofcom, 13 May 2026** — [pimpbunny/kemono investigations](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/ofcom-investigates-two-porn-sites-under-age-check-rules): action was “prioritised… based on the risk of harm… including where we have seen **significant increases in their user traffic** since age-check laws came into force.”

**Status:** Live enforcement gap. Fines and geo-blocking are the practical remedies; ISP blocking unused; recovery against offshore operators uncertain.

**Gaps/unknowns:** How many of the 31 fined services actually paid; how many continue to operate from new domains; whether Ofcom has sought court orders and been refused; the scale of domain-hopping/re-branding after enforcement.

### 1.4 App stores, operating systems and device-level assurance are outside the duty

**Description:** The Act regulates services, not the app-store/OS/device layer. Ofcom’s statutory app-store report (29 October 2026) says app stores could do more but are not regulated; the Government is still deciding whether to bring them into scope. Device-level age signals are emerging voluntarily (Apple iOS 26), but responsibility remains with the regulated service.

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, 29 October 2026** — [*Report on the use of app stores by children*](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/report-on-the-use-of-app-stores-by-children): “**App stores are not currently regulated services under the Act.**” The report finds app stores “did not consistently use age assurance to correctly establish whether a user was a child, providing little incentive for children to self-declare an accurate age.”
- **Ofcom, 29 October 2026** — same report, para 2.27/note 14: the analysis “did not include app stores primarily associated with devices such as **smart TVs and wearables**, as these provide more limited access to regulated services under the Act.” This is an explicit boundary of the evidence base.
- **Ofcom, 29 October 2026** — [*Rapid assessment of HEAA at 16*](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/rapid-assessment-of-highly-effective-age-assurance-at-16): “**No age assurance method is completely resistant to circumvention**”; services will need “practical steps to identify and address attempts by children to bypass age checks.”
- **Ofcom, 29 October 2026** — [*How age checks at 16 can work…*](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/how-age-checks-at-age-16-can-work-to-underpin-uks-social-media-ban): four methods capable of being highly effective at 16 — photo ID, digital ID, banking checks, facial age estimation; app stores “could play an important role” but “responsibility… remains with regulated services.”
- **Ofcom, 23 September 2026** — [Aylo investigation](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/investigation-into-aylos-compliance-with-the-duty-to-prevent-children-from-encountering-pornographic-content-through-the-use-of-age-assurance): Pornhub’s May 2026 process “relies on signals from a third party, in this case **Apple**”; “The Online Safety Act is clear that it is the **service provider’s responsibility** to ensure that any age assurance process is highly effective — no matter at what stage an age check occurs.”
- **House of Commons, 21 September 2026** — [PQ 26106](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-09-01/26106): “We recognise the potential merits of safety protections at device-level… However, **responsibility for compliance… remains with regulated services**.”

**Status:** Live gap; policy under review. App-store inclusion is a possible future SI; device-level is voluntary/contractual.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether the Government will bring app stores into scope in the under-16 ban regulations or a separate bill; whether Ofcom can effectively test OS-level signals; whether Apple/Google will provide age tokens to porn sites at scale; whether smart TVs/VR are deliberately left out.

### 1.5 Technical bypass and the limits of “highly effective” assurance

**Description:** Even compliant sites can be bypassed with low-cost techniques: photo-mode avatars, deepfakes, borrowed accounts, cookie replay, client-side gating, and VPNs. Academic testing suggests much deployment is “compliance theatre” rather than robust access control.

**What we found**

- **Lavermicocca, Carminati & Longari, arXiv, 7 June 2026** — [*X-rated Compliance Theater*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08667): study of 154 adult sites in the UK, France, Germany and Italy. UK: **69/154** sites had age verification; **60** used age estimation, **14** ID, **17** email, **5** SMS, **28** credit card. The authors find “systemic weaknesses across mechanisms and integrations under realistic threat assumptions, including failures against low-cost, widely accessible attacks.”
- **The Verge, 28 June 2025** — reported that Reddit and Discord’s UK age verification could be defeated using **Death Stranding’s photo mode**; cited in **Bileta written evidence to Ofcom, 2026** — [PDF](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/consultations/category-3-4-weeks/call-for-evidence-statutory-reports-on-age-assurance-and-app-stores/responses/bileta.pdf).
- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026** — age assurance report: “Most analysed services had taken steps to mitigate circumvention, including the use of **liveness detection** alongside facial age estimation and/or photo ID matching.” But Ofcom also says “circumvention appears low, although some activity may not be captured due to **reporting bias**.”
- **DSIT, 14 July 2026** — [*Children’s circumvention behaviours online*](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/childrens-circumvention-behaviours-online): 45% of children had encountered “advanced” checks; 57% had seen a tick box and 56% a date-of-birth entry; children rated government ID upload as most effective.
- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026** — age assurance report: “Some of the social media services that children use the most continue to rely on **proprietary age inference systems**, which we did not include in our HEAA guidance.” TikTok was subsequently put under investigation.

**Status:** Live technical gap; enforcement focus shifting from “no check” to “check not highly effective” (e.g. Aylo, Bit Hive/eporner).

**Gaps/unknowns:** Independent, reproducible testing of vendor claims; how Ofcom will assess “liveness” and injection attacks; whether reusable age tokens will be bound to devices/users; whether the “compliance theatre” critique is accepted by courts.

### 1.6 Payment processors and affiliate/ad networks are outside the statutory regime

**Description:** Payment processors and adult ad/affiliate networks are not regulated by the OSA, yet they exercise significant gatekeeping power. The Bertin review called payment providers the “unofficial, unelected regulators” of pornography. Their rules are inconsistent, opaque and not a substitute for statutory age assurance.

**What we found**

- **Baroness Bertin, 27 February 2025** — [*Creating a Safer World*](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/creating-a-safer-world-the-challenge-of-regulating-online-pornography), p 99: “**Payment providers are viewed as the unofficial regulators of online pornography**, using their power of withdrawing their services… The Review was able to view some of these prohibited content lists, which are **not accessible to the public**… these lists are **not consistent nor universal** across payment providers.”
- **Bertin review**, p 6: “the closest to any kind of regulator the online pornography industry has is **payment processors**.”
- **Bertin review**, Recommendation 12: “Increased, effective, and quick **business disruption measures across the ecosystem of pornography — including ancillary services** that support the platforms — should be in place.”
- **Visa, October 2026** — [Merchant Data Standards Manual](https://usa.visa.com/dam/VCOM/download/merchants/visa-merchant-data-standards-manual.pdf): MCC **5967** “Adult Content and Services” is a “high integrity risk” category; adult content merchants are subject to enhanced due diligence.
- **Mastercard AN 5196** (2021; industry reports of 2026 updates) — requires documented consent, age and identity verification for performers, pre-publication review and takedown. Secondary summary: [Bidda, 3 June 2026](https://bidda.com/intelligence/payment-processing-restricted-content-visa-virp-mastercard-an-5196): “payment-network compliance is **not a substitute** for statutory compliance.”
- **Patreon, updated 29 September 2026** — [Age Verification FAQ](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/8335062384141-Age-Verification-Frequently-Asked-Questions-FAQ): “Payment processors like Mastercard and Visa require Patreon to have **all creators** sharing sexually explicit creations… verify that they are over the age of 18.” The published requirement is creator-side; no equivalent HEAA viewer gate is described.
- **TrafficJunky, 23 July 2026** — [Rules & Regulations](https://www.trafficjunky.com/rules-and-regulations): in age-verification jurisdictions, ads must be SFW; “Any adult… content may only be presented after the User reaches the Advertiser’s landing page… which must implement a compliant age-verification process.” [Terms of Use](https://www.trafficjunky.com/terms-of-use): “TrafficJunky **does not operate, host, control, or monitor** the Buyer’s landing pages or destinations, and assumes no responsibility or liability for their compliance.”
- **TrafficJunky** is the ad network of **Aylo Freesites Ltd** (Pornhub’s parent), selling “133M+ users daily” on Pornhub and its network — a vertical integration between a regulated porn provider and an unregulated ad network.
- **ExoClick, 2026** — [Age Verification docs](https://docs.exoclick.com/exoclick-docs/age-verification-benefits): publishers can tag traffic “Verified” or “Not Verified (SFW)”; “This setting supports your compliance efforts but **does not by itself guarantee legal compliance**.”

**Status:** Live gap. No statutory regulation of payment processors or ad/affiliate networks; Bertin’s ancillary-services recommendation unimplemented.

**Gaps/unknowns:** How often adult sites use offshore/high-risk processors; whether card-network rules are enforced against UK-facing sites; whether affiliate landing pages are age-gated in practice; whether “SFW” ad placements still link to unprotected adult destinations.

### 1.7 VPNs and encrypted/private channels

**Description:** VPNs are lawful and cannot be banned; the Government’s approach is to put the onus on platforms to detect circumvention. Encrypted messaging and private channels are a separate gap: age assurance at the door does not reach content shared inside Telegram channels, Discord servers or private messages.

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026** — age assurance report: **5%** of children in its VPN study said they had used a VPN in the past six months to access age-restricted content; **7%** of 13–17-year-olds reported using a VPN and seeing explicit sexual content in a three-month period (PQ HL2344, 30 July 2026).
- **House of Commons, 30 July 2026** — [PQ HL2344](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-07-20/HL2344): “Government-commissioned research found that **7% of 13- to 17-year-olds** reported using a VPN and seeing explicit sexual content online in a three-month period. The research also found that **VPNs are not the most common method** by which children bypass age restrictions.”
- **HCWS254, 15 July 2026** — [statement](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-statements/detail/2026-07-15/hcws254): “VPNs have legitimate privacy and security uses and we will therefore **not age-gate or ban them**. Instead, we will put an onus on platforms… to take robust steps to detect and prevent attempts by underage users to circumvent age assurance measures.”
- **Edinburgh/Durham/Newcastle study, 15 October 2026** — [University of Edinburgh news](https://www.ed.ac.uk/news/privacy-concerns-amid-growing-vpn-interest): UK user discussions of VPNs rose sharply around 25 July 2025; the study warns users seeking to avoid ID disclosure may move to “high-risk VPN services [that] track, retain and share user data with third parties.” The paper is [arXiv 2606.05273](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05273).
- **Ofcom, 21 April 2026** — [Telegram investigation](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/ofcom-investigates-telegram-and-teen-chat-sites): probe into whether Telegram is doing enough to prevent CSAM sharing; separate investigations into Teen Chat and Chat Avenue over grooming.
- **Ofcom, 20 May 2026** — [Children’s Passive Online Measurement](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/online-safety/research-statistics-and-data/protecting-children/childrens-passive-online-measurement-report-2.pdf): “**Telegram was visited by 10% of 8-14s**, despite a minimum age requirement of 18.”
- **Commons Library, 28 October 2026** — [CBP-10979](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10979/): section 121/122 technology notices and end-to-end encryption remain contested; ORG calls s 122 the “spy clause”; the Government says E2EE “does not exempt platforms from their obligations to protect children from CSA.”

**Status:** Live and partly accepted. VPN use is a minority but non-trivial route; platform detection duties are emerging, not statutory. Encrypted/private channels remain largely outside age-gating.

**Gaps/unknowns:** How platforms can detect VPN use without undermining privacy; whether Ofcom will require VPN-detection as a code measure; whether Telegram’s “age controls” cover private channels; the scale of porn sharing in E2EE services.

## 2. Platform and sector-specific gaps

### 2.1 Pornhub/Aylo and Apple’s device-level check

**Description:** Aylo blocked new UK users in February 2026, then partially reopened in May 2026 for iPhone/iPad users who had completed Apple’s device-level age check. Ofcom is investigating whether Aylo’s reliance on Apple’s signal is “highly effective” and whether it carried out a suitable children’s access assessment.

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, 23 September 2026** — [Aylo investigation](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/investigation-into-aylos-compliance-with-the-duty-to-prevent-children-from-encountering-pornographic-content-through-the-use-of-age-assurance): “In May 2026, Pornhub deployed a new age assurance process for some users. This process relies on signals from a third party, in this case **Apple**… We are concerned that Aylo may not have conducted sufficient due diligence and testing.”
- **BBC News, 23 September 2026** — [*Pornhub investigated over its age checks*](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6eq8dw3dlyqo): Aylo argues Apple’s system “provides significantly stronger protection than any other age assurance method currently available in the UK market”; AVPA’s Iain Corby doubts Aylo can show “that **each time** someone accesses its site, the person is the same one who originally proved their age.”
- **The Register, 23 September 2026** — [report](https://assets.theregister.com/2026/09/23/202614): Aylo restricted Pornhub to new UK users on **2 February 2026**; UK visits fell 47% shortly after the rules and were “reportedly down **77%** by October”; in May it partially reversed the restriction for eligible Apple users; Android/PC users remain locked out.
- **Guardian, 23 September 2026** — [report](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2026/sep/23/pornhub-investigation-ofcom-online-safety-act-children): Ofcom is investigating “Pornhub’s use of Apple’s age verification process”; Aylo says device-level checks are “so-called device-level checks.”
- **Ofcom, 23 September 2026** — Ofcom “will **not** investigate how Apple operates its age checks”; the probe concerns Aylo’s implementation and testing.

**Status:** Open investigation (opened 22 September 2026). No decision yet.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether Ofcom will accept device-level signals as HEAA when combined with service-level checks; whether Apple’s age token is reusable across sites and accounts; whether Aylo will be found to have breached s 12/s 36; whether other porn sites will follow Aylo’s device-level model.

### 2.2 OnlyFans

**Description:** OnlyFans is a subscription/creator platform with adult content. Its age-assurance mechanism uses facial age estimation with a configurable “challenge age”; Ofcom fined its parent £1.05m in March 2025 for misreporting that threshold, not for a child-access breach. The platform has also been criticised because users can create an account and see adult advertisements before completing verification.

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, March 2025** — fine of **£1.05m** on Fenix International Ltd for providing inaccurate information about its age-estimation “challenge age” (reported as **20** in practice, **23** as reported to Ofcom). Secondary: [London Daily](https://londondaily.com/onlyfans-fined-1-million-for-misleading-information-responses); [VerifiedHer platform record](https://verifiedher.com/platforms/onlyfans).
- **OnlyFans Transparency Centre** — [Age & Identity Verification](https://onlyfans.com/transparency-center/verification): fans “may need to… complete third party age estimation/verification checks to view explicit media content”; the platform says it complies with age-verification requirements “in the jurisdictions in which it operates.”
- **Hwb (Welsh Government), updated 10 February 2026** — [OnlyFans guide](https://hwb.gov.wales/keeping-safe-online/parents-and-carers/app-guides-for-parents-and-carers/only-fans): “users can still **create an account and access the site without verifying their age**. They will be unable to access any full content, but they will be **exposed to inappropriate advertisements** for adult content.”
- **Ofcom** reportedly closed a separate investigation into OnlyFans’ measures to prevent under-18 access; the £1.05m fine concerned information accuracy (London Daily, above).

**Status:** Live platform; age assurance in place; information-accuracy enforcement concluded. No current Ofcom child-access investigation found.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether the “challenge age” of 20 is sufficient to absorb facial-estimation error for 17-year-olds; whether account creation without verification exposes children to adult ads; whether creator-side verification is robust for performers’ ages/consent; whether OnlyFans’ web-only status (not on major app stores) reduces app-store leverage.

### 2.3 Reddit

**Description:** Reddit is a U2U service with large NSFW communities. It asks for a birthdate when users access mature content; only users flagged as under-13 must verify. The ICO fined Reddit £14.47m under UK GDPR in February 2026 for failing to apply robust age assurance and for processing under-13 data without a lawful basis — a data-protection action, not an OSA age-assurance finding.

**What we found**

- **ICO, 23 February 2026** — [Reddit, Inc. enforcement](https://ico.org.uk/action-weve-taken/enforcement/2026/02/reddit-inc): £14,472,500 penalty for infringing UK GDPR Articles 5(1)(a), 6, 8 and 35; Reddit “**failed to apply any robust age assurance mechanism** and therefore did not have a lawful basis for processing the personal information of children under the age of 13”; failed to carry out a DPIA before January 2025.
- **Reddit Help, updated 24 June 2026** — [UK Online Safety Act](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/47163677520276-United-Kingdom-Online-Safety-Act): “Reddit will ask for your **birthdate** when you attempt to access mature (NSFW) or restricted content. **If we have information indicating you are under 13, you must verify your age** to continue using your account.” Verification options: Apple account (iOS) or Persona (selfie/ID).
- **ICO** — Reddit lodged an appeal with the First-tier Tribunal on **1 April 2026**; outcome not established (secondary: [iTechGuides, 18 August 2026](https://www.itechguides.com/why-the-uk-fined-reddit-14-47m-over-childrens-data-and-age-checks)).
- **Ofcom, 8 May 2026** — [*Making life safer online*](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/making-life-safer-online-our-priorities-for-the-year-ahead): Reddit is among platforms that “have also introduced age checks.”

**Status:** Live; ICO penalty under appeal; Ofcom treats Reddit as having age controls. The OSA-specific adequacy of Reddit’s birthdate gate is not publicly resolved.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether a self-declared birthdate followed by NSFW gating satisfies HEAA for pornographic subreddits; whether Ofcom has assessed Reddit’s age assurance; whether the ICO appeal changes the compliance baseline; how Reddit handles VPN/throwaway accounts.

### 2.4 X (formerly Twitter)

**Description:** X introduced age controls and is subject to Ofcom scrutiny over Grok-generated sexualised imagery, including images of children. The X case illustrates that age assurance and illegal-content duties are separate: a platform can have age checks and still be investigated for illegal content generation/distribution.

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, January 2026** — [investigation into X over Grok sexualised imagery](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/ofcom-launches-investigation-into-x-over-grok-sexualised-imagery): formal probe into whether X has complied with duties to protect UK users from illegal content.
- **CCDH written evidence to the Lords, 2026** — [IOS0110](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/169585/html): analysis of 4.6 million images posted by the Grok account between 29 December 2025 and 8 January 2026 estimated “approximately **three million were photorealistic sexualised images** and around **23,000 appeared to depict children**”; 29% of the sexualised images of children identified were still publicly accessible on X by 15 January 2026.
- **Ofcom, 8 May 2026** — X is among platforms that “have also introduced age checks” (Ofcom priorities page).
- **Online Safety Act Network, 12 January 2026** — [*Ofcom launches investigation into X: what happens next?*](https://www.onlinesafetyact.net/analysis/ofcom-launches-investigation-into-x-what-happens-next): the Grok case shows the limits of a content-by-content approach; the Network calls for safety-by-design and AI chatbot duties.

**Status:** Open Ofcom investigation; X has age controls but the Grok issue is illegal-content/risk-assessment, not HEAA.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether X’s age checks are HEAA for pornographic content; whether Grok-generated CSAM/sexualised imagery is being prevented at source; whether the investigation will produce a fine or require design changes.

### 2.5 Telegram

**Description:** Telegram is an encrypted/private messaging service with public channels that can host porn and CSAM. Ofcom opened a CSAM investigation in April 2026; Telegram has introduced age controls, but its private-channel architecture makes door-level age assurance ineffective.

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, 21 April 2026** — [Telegram and teen chat sites investigation](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/ofcom-investigates-telegram-and-teen-chat-sites): probe into whether Telegram is complying with duties to prevent CSAM sharing; separate probes into Teen Chat and Chat Avenue over grooming.
- **The Next Web, 21 April 2026** — [report](https://thenextweb.com/news/ofcom-telegram-investigation-csam-online-safety-act): Ofcom’s “most significant enforcement action against a major messaging platform to date”; Telegram had introduced age controls in response to the OSA.
- **Ofcom, 20 May 2026** — [Children’s Passive Online Measurement](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/online-safety/research-statistics-and-data/protecting-children/childrens-passive-online-measurement-report-2.pdf): “Telegram was visited by **10% of 8-14s**, despite a minimum age requirement of 18.”
- **Ofcom, 8 May 2026** — Telegram is among platforms that “have also introduced age checks.”

**Status:** Open CSAM investigation; age controls in place but private/encrypted channels remain a structural gap.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether Telegram’s age checks apply to channel owners/viewers or only account creation; whether Ofcom can obtain data from Telegram; whether private channels are within the scope of any age-assurance duty; the scale of porn-sharing in Telegram groups.

### 2.6 AI companions and sexualised chatbots

**Description:** AI companion/chatbot services can generate pornographic text/images and sexual role-play. Ofcom says a chatbot is in scope if it meets the definitions of a U2U, search or pornographic service; the Government plans to require age assurance for sexualised features by 2027. The gap is that general-purpose chatbots are not age-gated, and “romantic companion” apps may fall between categories.

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, 15 January 2026** — [Novi Ltd investigation](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/ofcom-investigates-ai-companion-chatbot-service): probe into an “AI character companion chatbot service” over age-check requirements; Ofcom had launched investigations into **94 sites and apps** in total by then.
- **Ofcom, 18 December 2025** — [*AI chatbots and online regulation*](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/ai-chatbots-and-online-regulation-what-you-need-to-know): a chatbot is covered if it meets the OSA definitions, including “services that publish pornographic images, video or audio online, including via chatbots — they must use highly effective age assurance.”
- **DSIT, June 2026** — [*Growing up in the online world: progress statement*](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/growing-up-in-the-online-world-a-national-consultation/outcome/growing-up-in-the-online-world-progress-statement): “AI chatbot services whose primary purpose is to provide sexual relationships to their users will **not be available to children under 18**”; general-purpose chatbots will not be age-gated, but “to access features that enable sexually explicit interaction, a user would need to prove they are over 18.”
- **IWF, May 2026** — [consultation response](https://www.iwf.org.uk/media/pbbhjedv/iwf-consultation-response-plus-csea-sector-recommendations-june2026.pdf): “Currently, chatbot services are **not adequately covered** by the Online Safety Act, which only includes user-to-user, search and pornographic services.” IWF calls for standalone chatbot duties.
- **OSA Network, January 2026** — [10-point plan](https://www.onlinesafetyact.net/analysis/strengthening-the-osa-our-10-point-plan-for-government): calls for “a new series of standalone duties for AI chatbot services to address the emerging harms that currently fall out of scope of the OSA.”

**Status:** Live enforcement (Novi); policy commitment for 2027; no standalone chatbot duties yet.

**Gaps/unknowns:** How Ofcom will classify “companion” vs “general-purpose” chatbots; whether image-generation features are age-gated separately; whether the 2027 regime will cover text-only sexual role-play; how age assurance works for anonymous chatbot users.

### 2.7 Nudify apps and non-consensual intimate image generators

**Description:** Nudify apps/websites create synthetic intimate images. The Crime and Policing Act 2026 bans making/supplying purported intimate image generators; the EU has moved to ban nudifier apps; Australia’s eSafety has taken action. But distribution via app stores, Telegram bots and offshore sites remains a whack-a-mole problem.

**What we found**

- **ISD, July 2026** — [*Mapping the ‘Nudify’ Tools Ecosystem*](https://www.isdglobal.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Mapping-the-Nudify-Tools-Ecosystem.pdf): of **181** nudify sites identified, **163 appeared accessible from the UK** on 1 April 2026; “only **two** included measures to verify that users were over 18”; **61** required a click “yes”; “the remaining **100 websites did not include any disclaimer or age verification requirement**.” 88% used Cloudflare/Let’s Encrypt.
- **WIRED, 2 November 2026** — [*Hundreds of Mundane AI Apps Are Hiding Sexually Abusive Deepfake Features*](https://www.wired.com/story/hundreds-of-mundane-ai-apps-are-hiding-abusive-deepfake-features/): “bait-and-switch” apps published via Apple/Google; the company says it has suspended “tens of thousands of apps” and updated app-store rules.
- **Crime and Policing Act 2026, s 99** — [factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/crime-and-policing-act-2026-factsheets/crime-and-policing-act-2026-intimate-image-abuse-factsheet): ban on “intimate image generators,” including “nudification apps, websites, nudification bots used on social media channels such as **Telegram**, and AI models.”
- **European Parliament, 16 June 2026** — [press release](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/pdfs/news/expert/2026/6/press_release/20260611IPR45207/20260611IPR45207_en.pdf): AI Act amendment bans AI systems that generate CSAM or non-consensual intimate images of identifiable people, unless adequate safeguards.
- **eSafety, 19 May 2026** — [action against a major nudify service](https://www.esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/esafety-takes-action-against-another-major-nudify-service-for-failing-to-protect-australian-children): first enforcement action under the Age-Restricted Material Codes for systemic non-compliance; service had nearly 40,000 Australian visits/month.

**Status:** Live criminal-law reform (UK), but enforcement against offshore apps/bots is nascent. No dedicated OSA age-assurance duty for nudify tools as such.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether the s 99 offences are being enforced; whether app stores are removing apps proactively; whether Telegram bots are within UK jurisdiction; whether “art/education” or open-source model defences apply; the scale of UK use.

### 2.8 Dating apps

**Description:** Dating apps are Part 3 U2U services and can host pornographic content or facilitate adult-minor contact. Ofcom says the sector has introduced age checks, but some 15–17-year-olds still use the most popular apps, and age inference/self-declaration remain issues.

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026** — age assurance report: dating apps are one of the sectors examined; the report says age checks “needed implementing more effectively, including by dating services” (Commons Library CBP-10979 summary).
- **Ofcom, 8 May 2026** — [priorities page](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/making-life-safer-online-our-priorities-for-the-year-ahead): “dating apps like **Tinder, Grindr, Bumble and Hinge** are also now using age checks to prevent inappropriate contact between adults and under-18s.”
- **Tinder, 13 August 2026** — [Age Checks at Tinder](https://www.help.tinder.com/hc/en-us/articles/41083211035917-Age-Checks-at-Tinder): in the UK, a **face photo upload** age check plus a possible **ID verification** check; “in some regions… we may receive age information provided by the Apple App Store or Google Play Store.”
- **Grindr** — [Age Assurance on Grindr](https://help.grindr.com/hc/en-us/articles/47133656615827-Age-Assurance-on-Grindr): UK users “must complete a **one-time Age Assurance check**”; FaceTec estimates whether the user appears 18+; if not, account may be restricted/removed.
- **ProofAge, 17 July 2026** — [analysis](https://proofage.net/blog/uk-age-verification-dating-apps): “In December 2025, **more than one in ten 15 to 17-year-olds** in the UK were still visiting and spending time on three of the most used dating apps. The figure is Ofcom’s, published on 15 July 2026.”

**Status:** Live; major apps have checks; enforcement not yet focused on dating apps.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether face-based checks are HEAA for 17-year-olds; whether app-store age signals are accepted; whether smaller dating apps have any checks; how dating apps handle adult-minor contact beyond age gating.

### 2.9 Games, VR and smart TVs

**Description:** Gaming platforms and consoles have introduced age checks, but adult games and VR porn are less clearly covered. Ofcom’s app-store report expressly excludes smart TVs and wearables, and VR is not addressed in the UK evidence base. This is a “device/peripheral” blind spot.

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, 8 May 2026** — priorities page: “Major platforms including X, Telegram, Discord, Reddit, Bluesky, **Xbox and Steam** have also introduced age checks”; “**Roblox** now uses age checks to restrict adults from contacting children they’re not already connected to.”
- **Ofcom, 29 October 2026** — app-store report: “we did not include app stores primarily associated with devices such as **smart TVs and wearables**, as these provide more limited access to regulated services under the Act.”
- **Mashable, 24 July 2025** — [*Adult games are disappearing from platforms like Steam*](https://sea.mashable.com/tech/38724/adult-games-are-disappearing-from-platforms-like-steam): “Platforms are bowing down to pressure from **payment processors**”; Steam and itch.io deindexed/removed adult-only games after pressure from Collective Shout.
- **DevRunners, 12 September 2026** — [game-store age verification guide](https://devrunners.com/blog/game-store-age-verification-guide): Australia’s app-distribution obligations commenced 9 September 2026; California’s app-store law (signed 15 May 2026, effective 1 July 2027) covers “smartphone, tablet, computer, gaming console or **VR device**.” (Secondary industry tracker.)

**Status:** Partial coverage. Major consoles/games platforms have checks; VR and smart-TV app stores are outside Ofcom’s app-store evidence base and not clearly regulated.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether VR porn apps/sites are in scope and age-gated; whether smart-TV browsers/apps can bypass service-level checks; whether console stores verify age at purchase or at use; whether adult games are simply deindexed rather than age-gated.

### 2.10 Piracy and leak sites

**Description:** Leak/piracy sites hosting user-generated porn are Part 3 services, but many operate offshore and grow when mainstream sites age-gate. Ofcom has prioritised several based on traffic increases; the cases show both the enforcement model and its limits.

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, 13 May 2026** — [pimpbunny.com and kemono.cr investigations](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/ofcom-investigates-two-porn-sites-under-age-check-rules): investigations opened; Ofcom “taken particular account of their user numbers, including where we have seen **significant increases in their user traffic** since age-check laws came into force.”
- **Ofcom, 9 July 2026** — [fapello.com](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/ofcom-fines-porn-company-630000-for-age-check-failings): £600,000 for no age checks; fapello subsequently geo-blocked the UK.
- **Ofcom, 4 September 2026** — [Xgroovy](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/porn-site-deploys-age-checks-as-ofcom-fines-it-730000): £700,000 plus £30,000; provider introduced age assurance then geo-blocked.
- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026** — age assurance report: “Many porn sites that have introduced age checks have experienced **sharp declines in user traffic**, while some sites **without age assurance have gained in popularity**.”

**Status:** Live enforcement; leak sites are a moving target; geo-blocking is a common outcome.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether leak sites re-register under new domains; whether payment/affiliate networks continue to serve them; whether search engines downrank them; whether victims’ takedown routes work across clones.

### 2.11 Creator platforms, text and “art”

**Description:** Creator/subscription platforms (Patreon, OnlyFans, Substack) blur the line between U2U, provider porn and text. Patreon’s published age verification is creator-side (payment-processor driven); Substack allows erotic literature and artistic nudity. These are plausible avoidance routes for the Part 5 “provider porn” model.

**What we found**

- **Patreon, updated 29 September 2026** — [Age Verification FAQ](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/8335062384141-Age-Verification-Frequently-Asked-Questions-FAQ): payment processors require Patreon to have “**all creators** sharing sexually explicit creations or creations that include nudity verify that they are over the age of 18.” The FAQ is about creators, not viewers.
- **Substack, 29 September 2026** — [Content Guidelines](https://substack.com/content): prohibits porn but allows “depictions of nudity for artistic, journalistic, or related purposes, as well as **erotic literature**.”
- **Ofcom, 13 December 2024** — *Overview of regulated services*: provider porn excludes content “that consists **only of text**.”
- **Ofcom, 24 April 2025** — *Guidance on Content Harmful to Children*: “If the **primary purpose is artistic**… content will not meet the definition under the Act.”

**Status:** Uncertain/live. No enforcement action found against Patreon or Substack for age assurance.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether Patreon age-gates viewers at all; whether Substack’s erotic literature is treated as pornographic content; whether text-only content is a growing route as AI-generated erotic text/audio expands; whether payment-processor creator checks are being conflated with viewer age assurance.

## 3. The Independent Review of Pornography (Baroness Bertin, 2025)

**Description:** The Independent Review of Pornography, led by Baroness Bertin, published *Creating a Safer World — the Challenge of Regulating Online Pornography* on 27 February 2025 (HC 592). It made **32 recommendations** across VAWG, platform accountability, exploitation, enforcement, tech-enabled harms and governance. The Government published a short joint letter response the same day, promising a fuller response; as of late 2025 it had not provided a timetable.

**What we found**

- **Bertin review, 27 February 2025** — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/creating-a-safer-world-the-challenge-of-regulating-online-pornography); [PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67c0802016dc9038974dbc71/HC_592_The_Challenge_of_Regulating_Online_Pornography.pdf). Key recommendations:
  - **Rec 1:** harmful porn that is illegal to distribute offline should be illegal online (a “Safe Pornography Code” or new publication offence).
  - **Rec 2:** non-fatal strangulation porn illegal to possess/distribute/publish.
  - **Rec 3:** non-consensual taking/making of intimate images (real or deepfake) an offence.
  - **Rec 9:** separate body to conduct content audits.
  - **Rec 10:** accreditation scheme for compliant platforms, visible to banks/payment providers.
  - **Rec 12:** business disruption measures across the **ancillary services** ecosystem (advertisers, payment providers, data centres, media owners).
  - **Rec 13:** review regulators with a view to a **single focused online safety regulator**.
  - **Rec 15:** an ombudsman for intimate image abuse/trafficking victims.
  - **Rec 21:** mandatory performer/creator age and consent verification.
  - **Rec 29:** ban nudify apps.
  - **Rec 31:** clear Whitehall ownership of pornography policy.
- **Bertin review, p 6:** “the closest to any kind of regulator the online pornography industry has is **payment processors**.” It urges Ofcom/Government to look at “**device-level age verification**” if systems are not effective enough.
- **Bertin review, p 99:** payment providers’ prohibited-content lists are “**not accessible to the public**,” “**not consistent nor universal**,” and “large volumes of violent and harmful content is still available online.”
- **Government response, 27 February 2025** — [letter](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/response-to-the-independent-review-of-pornography-letter-to-baroness-bertin/letter-to-baroness-bertin-government-response-to-the-independent-pornography-review): “We will carefully consider each of the recommendations… and provide a fuller response to each in due course”; highlights immediate action on age assurance and intimate image abuse.
- **PQ HL10894, answered 27 October 2025** — [link](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2025-10-13/HL10894): “we are **not yet able to provide a timeline** for the Government’s response.”
- **PQ HL11790, answered 3 December 2025** — [link](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2025-11-11/HL11790): recommendations relevant to VAWG are being considered in the **VAWG Strategy**; “further update… in due course.”
- **Crime and Policing Act 2026 pornography factsheet, 29 September 2026** — [link](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/crime-and-policing-act-2026-factsheets/crime-and-policing-act-2026-pornography-factsheet): the Government’s “Joint Team on pornography” is conducting a **6-month sprint** into regulation of online pornography and “how to achieve parity between off-line and on-line.”
- **Crime and Policing Act 2026, ss 108–110** — [Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/20): a duty to review **age and consent verification** for individuals appearing in pornographic content, and a power to amend the OSA to impose such duties. This is the most direct legislative vehicle for Bertin’s performer-verification recommendations.

**Status:** Partially implemented. Nudify ban (Rec 29), strangulation/incest/under-16 role-play offences (Recs 2, 25 and related) have been legislated via the Crime and Policing Act 2026. The rest — including Rec 1’s “Safe Pornography Code,” Rec 12’s ancillary-services disruption, Rec 13’s single regulator, Rec 15’s ombudsman, and Rec 21’s performer verification — remain unimplemented or under review.

**Gaps/unknowns:** No full Government response or implementation plan; no timetable; no published cost/benefit analysis; unclear whether the “6-month sprint” will produce legislation in the current Parliament; no evidence yet on the s 108–110 review.

### 4.4 Public Accounts Committee and National Audit Office

### 4.6 The statutory review under s 178 OSA

**Description:** The OSA contains its own review clause. Section 178 requires the Secretary of State to review the operation of the regulatory framework, including the effectiveness of Ofcom’s information and enforcement powers and the proportionality of regulation. The review must not be carried out before two years, and must be carried out before five years, after the last Part 3 provision comes into force.

**What we found**

- **Online Safety Act 2023, s 178** — [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/178): the review must consider “how effective the regulatory framework… is at… minimising the risk of harm… providing higher levels of protection for children than for adults… freedom of expression… privacy,” and “the effectiveness of… enforcement powers.”
- **Commons Library, 28 October 2026** — CBP-10979: the Act is “expected to be **fully implemented by mid-2027**.”
- **On that estimate**, the s 178 review window would open around **mid-2029** and close around **mid-2032**. No Government announcement of the review was found.
- **Lords inquiry** is a parliamentary inquiry, not the statutory review; it may inform it but does not satisfy s 178.

**Status:** Not started. The statutory clock is tied to the last Part 3 commencement, not to the porn duties (which began in 2025).

**Gaps/unknowns:** Which Part 3 provision is “last” (categorised services/transparency may slip); whether the Government will accelerate the review; whether the review will cover Part 5 porn duties specifically; how the Lords inquiry and s 178 review will interact.

### 4.8 Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 — not an age-verification reform

**Description:** The DUAA is sometimes mentioned as a possible vehicle for online safety changes. In fact it made only limited OSA amendments: child-death data retention, researcher access, and a children’s higher-protection provision in UK GDPR. It did **not** extend HEAA or create new age-assurance duties.

**What we found**

- **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, s 124** — [explanatory notes](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/notes/division/12/index.htm): amends the OSA to require retention of information by internet service providers in connection with the death of a child; inserts new s 100(8A).
- **DUAA, s 125** — inserts new **s 154A OSA**, a power to make regulations requiring providers to give information to researchers for online safety research.
- **DUAA, s 81** — amends UK GDPR Article 25 to require controllers of ISS likely to be accessed by children to take account of “children’s higher protection matters.”
- **ICO, 19 June 2026** — [DUAA guidance](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/legislation-we-cover/data-use-and-access-act-2025/the-data-use-and-access-act-2025-what-does-it-mean-for-organisations/): “if you provide an online service that is likely to be used by children, the DUAA explicitly requires you to take their needs into account when you decide how to use their personal information.”
- **No DUAA provision** was found that extends HEAA to search, app stores or new service categories.

**Status:** In force; implementation of the s 154A researcher-access regulations is a separate process.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether the researcher-access power will be commenced; whether the children’s higher-protection duty will be enforced by the ICO; whether DUAA will be used as a vehicle for future age-assurance changes (no evidence found).

### 4.9 Crime and Policing Act 2026 — the main porn reform vehicle

**Description:** The Crime and Policing Act 2026 is the most significant porn-specific reform since the OSA. It creates new offences (strangulation, incest, adults role-playing as under-16s), bans nudify tools, and gives the Secretary of State a review duty and power to impose age/consent verification duties on porn providers.

**What we found**

- **Crime and Policing Act 2026, ss 106–107** — [pornography factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/crime-and-policing-act-2026-factsheets/crime-and-policing-act-2026-pornography-factsheet): criminalises possession/publication of pornography portraying strangulation or suffocation, incest (including step-incest where a person is portrayed as under 18), and sexual activity with a child under 16 (adults role-playing as children). These become **priority offences** under the OSA.
- **Crime and Policing Act 2026, s 99** — [intimate image abuse factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/crime-and-policing-act-2026-factsheets/crime-and-policing-act-2026-intimate-image-abuse-factsheet): bans making/supplying purported intimate image generators, including nudify apps, websites, Telegram bots and AI models.
- **Crime and Policing Act 2026, ss 108–110** — [Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/20): s 108 imposes a duty to review **age and consent verification** for individuals appearing in pornographic content; s 109 gives a power to amend the OSA to impose age/consent verification duties; s 110 gives a power to make regulations for specified internet services.
- **Royal Assent:** 29 April 2026.

**Status:** Royal Assent; implementation/commencement ongoing. No regulations under ss 109–110 and no published s 108 review were found.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether the Government will use ss 109–110 to impose performer age/consent verification (Bertin Rec 21); how consent verification would work where consent is withdrawn; whether the new offences are being enforced; whether the nudify ban reaches offshore suppliers.

## 7. International comparison and regulatory diffusion

### 7.1 European Union

**What we found**

- **European Commission, 2026** — [*The EU approach to age verification*](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-age-verification): a harmonised, privacy-preserving age-verification app/blueprint; users prove “over 18” without revealing identity; rollout expected across the EU by end-2026; first movers include Denmark, France, Greece, Italy, Spain, Cyprus and Ireland.
- **European Commission, 18 May 2026** — [FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/eu-age-verification-solution): “While it may be technically possible to circumvent age verification techniques, for example by using a VPN, this does not undermine the value of the app. It will serve as an important barrier to significantly reduce exposure…”
- **European Commission, 24 September 2026** — [common approach](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-sets-out-common-approach-eu-wide-age-verification-technologies): DSA Article 28 guidelines recommend “effective age assurance methods that are accurate, reliable, robust, non-intrusive and non-discriminatory”; the **EU KIDS Act** proposal (adopted September 2026) sets **15** as the age for autonomous social media accounts.
- **European Parliament, 2026** — resolution on protection of minors: notes “disparities in current age assurance measures… leading to a **fragmentation of the internal market**”; calls for harmonised EU age assurance.

**Status:** EU is building a public age-verification infrastructure; the UK’s private-market model is different.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether the EU app will be usable for porn sites; whether Member States will mandate it; whether the EU KIDS Act will set a 15+ default; whether the UK will recognise EU age credentials.

---

### 7.2 Australia

**What we found**

- **eSafety, 10 December 2025** — under-16 social media minimum age commenced. [eSafety hub](https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions): platforms must take “reasonable steps” to prevent under-16s having accounts; VPN circumvention must be addressed.
- **eSafety, March 2026** — [Compliance Update](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-03/SocialMediaMinimumAgeComplianceUpdateMarch2026.pdf): 4.7 million under-16 accounts removed by mid-December 2025; “significant numbers of children aged under 16 are still on social media”; no discernible drop in overall harm reports.
- **eSafety, August 2026** — [Early insights](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-08/Early-days-Early-Insights-SMMA-Evaluation-Understanding-experiences-of-social-media-age-restrictions-August2026.pdf): under-16 account holding fell from **52.4% to 42.1%**; use (with or without account) fell from **85.9% to 81.5%**; **37.1%** of under-16s listed their age as 16+; **18.2%** said the platform’s age check got their age wrong; VPN use was a minority route.
- **University of Newcastle / BMJ, 24 June 2026** — [news release](https://www.newcastle.edu.au/news/2026/06/australias-social-media-age-restrictions-show-limited-early-impact-new-research-finds): “more than **85 per cent** of adolescents under 16 continued to use restricted social media platforms at follow-up”; most used their own accounts; **66%** encountered some form of age verification; self-declared age (24–39%) and selfie (13–27%) were the most common methods; fake accounts 15–19%, someone else’s account 9–29%, private browser 11%.
- **AVPA, 26 June 2026** — [response](https://avpassociation.com/thought-leadership/bmj-publishes-research-confirming-age-assurance-is-not-being-deployed-in-australia): argues the BMJ study shows age assurance “barely got switched on,” not that it cannot work; VPN use was only 2–3%.

**Status:** Australia is the closest large-scale comparator; early results show modest reductions and significant circumvention, with implementation quality the key variable.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Longer-term effects; whether the UK’s “highly effective” standard will outperform Australia’s “reasonable steps”; whether the UK’s feature restrictions (livestreaming, stranger contact) will work; whether the Australian model’s exemptions (messaging, gaming) create displacement.

### 7.3 United States

**What we found**

- **US Supreme Court, 27 June 2025** — *Free Speech Coalition, Inc. v. Paxton* upheld Texas HB 1181, allowing states to require age verification for commercial porn sites. Secondary commentary: [Heritage Foundation](https://www.heritage.org/node/25158851/print-display): “no person—adult or child—has a First Amendment right to access [obscene] speech without first submitting proof of age.”
- **State laws:** by 2026, roughly **23–27 states** have active age-verification laws for adult sites (secondary: [MobiusPay, 18 June 2026](https://mobiuspay.com/blog/how-to-meet-age-verification-rules-for-adult-merchants); [Voxter, 31 July 2026](https://voxter.co.uk/)).

- **California app-store law** — signed 15 May 2026, effective 1 July 2027; covers app stores on phones, tablets, computers, consoles and **VR devices**; age category verified at account creation, parental consent before minor downloads (secondary: [DevRunners, 12 September 2026](https://devrunners.com/blog/game-store-age-verification-guide)).
- **Texas App Store Accountability Act** — in force; requires app stores to verify age at account creation (secondary: same).

**Status:** US is a patchwork; geo-blocking is common; state laws vary in methods and enforcement.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether the Supreme Court ruling will lead to a federal standard; whether app-store laws survive First Amendment challenges; whether US state laws cover VPNs; whether UK-style “highly effective” standards are being adopted.

### 7.4 “Brussels effect” and lessons

**What we found**

- **No source used the term “Brussels effect” specifically for the OSA.** The evidence shows **regulatory diffusion**: the UK was an early mover on HEAA; the EU is building a public age-verification app and DSA guidelines; Australia adopted an under-16 ban; US states adopted age-verification laws; California/Texas adopted app-store accountability.
- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026** — claims the UK is “at the forefront of global efforts” and that age checks are being deployed “at an unprecedented scale.”
- **Lessons from Australia:** implementation quality and enforcement matter more than the legal model; self-declaration and weak age checks produce limited impact; VPN use is a minority but real route; messaging/gaming exemptions create displacement.
- **Lessons from the EU:** public, privacy-preserving age credentials could reduce reliance on ID uploads; but rollout is slow and voluntary in parts.
- **Lessons from the US:** geo-blocking is a rational industry response; state patchworks create migration and VPN demand.

**Status:** Ongoing diffusion; no formal “Brussels effect” assessment.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether the UK will recognise EU age credentials; whether the EU KIDS Act will influence the UK’s under-16 regime; whether the UK’s model will be exported or isolated post-Brexit.

## 8. Industry behaviour change

### 8.1 Geo-blocking and site exits

**What we found**

- **Pornhub/Aylo:** blocked new UK users in February 2026; partially reopened for Apple users in May 2026; UK traffic reportedly down **77%** by October 2025 ([The Register](https://assets.theregister.com/2026/09/23/202614); [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6eq8dw3dlyqo)).
- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026:** **10** of the top 100 porn services geo-blocked the UK; **64** had age assurance; total **74%** inaccessible without circumvention (Ofcom press release).
- **Ofcom IOS0039, 7 September 2026:** **11** of the top 100 geo-blocked; **63** had age assurance; **74%** inaccessible.
- **Xgroovy** and **fapello** geo-blocked after enforcement ([Ofcom, 4 Sep 2026](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/porn-site-deploys-age-checks-as-ofcom-fines-it-730000); [Ofcom, 9 Jul 2026](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/ofcom-fines-porn-company-630000-for-age-check-failings)).
- **Imgur** stopped serving UK visitors on 30 September 2025 after ICO action over children’s data — an example of a platform exiting rather than complying (secondary: [vpnAlert](https://vpnalert.com/tools/censorship-monitor/uk/)).

**Status:** Geo-blocking is a stable industry response; it removes access for children but also for adults, and may displace demand.

**Gaps/unknowns:** How many smaller sites geo-block vs age-gate; whether geo-blocking is used to avoid scrutiny while serving UK users via VPN; whether Ofcom treats geo-blocking as compliance.

---

### 8.2 Traffic migration to unchecked sites

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026** — age assurance report: “Many porn sites that have introduced age checks have experienced **sharp declines in user traffic**, while some sites **without age assurance have gained in popularity**.”
- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026** — press release: “almost half (**47%**) of the pornography services visited by children have no age checks in place.”
- **Ofcom, 13 May 2026** — pimpbunny/kemono investigations: prioritised because of “**significant increases in their user traffic** since age-check laws came into force.”
- **Washington Post, 31 August 2025** — [*‘Scan your face’ laws for the web are having unexpected consequences*](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2025/08/31/age-verification-uk-porn-sites): age-verification laws brought “bursts of traffic to seedy parts of the web.”
- **AVPA IOS0037, 7 September 2026:** “some services without checks have **gained traffic**… children cannot simply move to unprotected services.”

**Status:** Clear displacement effect; the regime has not reduced overall availability, only shifted it.

**Gaps/unknowns:** The size of the migration; whether users return to age-gated sites after the initial shock; whether search engines amplify unchecked sites; whether migration is to piracy/leak sites or to mainstream platforms.

### 8.3 Search and discoverability

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026:** 33% of Google first-page results and 54% of Bing first-page results were to unprotected porn sites; Google and Bing agreed to “explore practical solutions.”
- **Ofcom CPOM, 14 July 2026:** search engines were ~30% of entry points; 62% of children who visited porn used search immediately before.
- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026** — age assurance report: “pornography services without age assurance **frequently appear in search queries**.”

**Status:** Voluntary workstream; no regulatory requirement.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether Google/Bing have changed ranking; whether Ofcom will use search codes; whether other search engines (DuckDuckGo, Yahoo) are covered.

### 8.4 VPN and circumvention

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026:** 5% of children used a VPN in the past six months to access age-restricted content; 7% of 13–17s reported VPN + explicit content (PQ HL2344).
- **Edinburgh/Durham/Newcastle, 15 October 2026:** sharp rise in UK VPN discussions around 25 July 2025; privacy risks from high-risk VPNs.
- **HCWS254, 15 July 2026:** Government will not ban VPNs; platforms must detect/prevent circumvention.
- **Ofcom, 29 October 2026** — [rapid assessment](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/rapid-assessment-of-highly-effective-age-assurance-at-16): “**No age assurance method is completely resistant to circumvention** and service providers will need to take practical steps to identify and address attempts by children to bypass age checks.”

**Status:** Accepted residual risk; no VPN ban; platform detection duties emerging.

**Gaps/unknowns:** How platforms detect VPNs without false positives; whether VPN detection is itself privacy-invasive; whether children use VPNs more than self-reported; whether VPN providers will be pressured.

### 8.5 Walled gardens, consolidation and content change

**What we found**

- **Ofcom, 15 July 2026:** top 10 porn services account for ~18% of UK traffic; top 100 ~0.5% of sites but >20% of visitors; the market is “dynamic” and the top 100 “changes often” (IOS0039).
- **Ofcom IOS0039:** 23 investigations covering 88 porn sites; roughly half concluded with sites “either blocking UK users or introducing age assurance.”
- **Bertin review / Crime and Policing Act 2026:** new offences for strangulation, incest and under-16 role-play porn; platforms must treat them as priority offences, which may lead to content removal rather than age-gating.
- **No reliable evidence found** of consolidation into a few “age-gated walled gardens,” nor of measurable content changes beyond enforcement-driven removals. This is a gap.

**Status:** Unclear. The market is consolidating around compliant large sites, but the long tail persists.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether age-gating creates a two-tier market (compliant “walled garden” vs unchecked long tail); whether creators migrate to platforms with weaker checks; whether content becomes more extreme on unchecked sites; whether consolidation increases or reduces harm.

### 8.6 Payment and affiliate pressure

**What we found**

- **Visa/Mastercard** rules require age/consent verification for adult merchants; this affects paid sites but not free sites.
- **Steam/itch.io** deindexed adult games after payment-processor pressure (Mashable, 24 July 2025).
- **Patreon** requires creator age verification due to payment processors (Patreon FAQ, 29 Sep 2026).
- **TrafficJunky/ExoClick** allow “verified” vs “not verified (SFW)” traffic targeting; TrafficJunky disclaims responsibility for landing pages.

**Status:** Private gatekeeping is shaping content availability more than the OSA in some segments.

**Gaps/unknowns:** Whether card networks will enforce against UK-facing free sites; whether ad networks will be brought into scope; whether payment pressure is a proxy for age assurance or a separate content-control regime.

## 11. Summary assessment

The evidence supports a report that treats the OSA porn regime as **necessary but structurally incomplete**. The strongest, best-evidenced weaknesses are:

1. **Search is outside HEAA** — a clear statutory gap, admitted by Ofcom, with quantitative evidence of unprotected results.
2. **Part 5 is narrow** — provider-only, text-excluded, art/education carve-outs; the “regulated provider pornographic content” category does not match the modern porn market.
3. **The long tail and offshore operators** — 20,000+ sites, 47% of child-visited services unchecked, zero ISP blocking, limited asset recovery.
4. **App stores/OS/devices** — outside the Act, with voluntary device-level signals and an unresolved Aylo probe.
5. **AI companions and nudify tools** — fast-growing, poorly covered, with weak age assurance and app-store/Telegram distribution.
6. **Ancillary infrastructure** — payment processors and ad/affiliate networks are private gatekeepers, not regulated utilities.
7. **Reform is slow and fragmented** — Bertin’s 32 recommendations largely unimplemented; the under-16 ban is the flagship; the s 178 review may not start until 2029.

The strongest counter-evidence is that the regime **has** changed behaviour: top sites age-gate or geo-block, 69 million checks were completed in six months, and most children who reach age-gated sites do not get past the gate. The report should present the regime as a partial success whose remaining weaknesses are increasingly structural and cross-border, not merely technical.
